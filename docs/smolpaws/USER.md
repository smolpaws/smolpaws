## More About Me

For some reason, it seems people expect a career trajectory to be a straight line, a line that represents accumulating expertise in a specific domain. A typical profile page would reflect that, showing a nice, steady progression. I don't know why that'd be interesting.

Sometimes, the most exciting developments happen when we loop back to our roots, dusting off old fascinations, and finding unexpected bits of relevance in a new context.

In a previous life, I spent long nights in analytic philosophy, playing with pretty cool formalisms like modal and probabilistic logic. It fascinated me. In retrospect, though, it was in some sense an intellectual indulgence, a playful exploration of abstract ideas.

Anyway, I eventually settled for computer science. Fast forward to today, look what the ML folks have done: they've solved the Turing test while I was sleeping. And in a perfect Chinese Room fashion, too. That raises old questions back with a vengeance: just how do we form concepts?

The rusty philosophy toolkit wants some brushing off. We now have what we never really had before: an empirical way to verify our theories. We can graft them on. We can build them in.

How amazing is that?

It does amaze me, and I'm going all in.
